1.Moral Skepticism 
-------------
- denies existence of objective moral standards 
- morality lacks authority 
- Ethical objectivism(on the other hand)
	 is the idea that some moral standards are objectively correct regardless of personal or cultural views
- two forms of moral skepticism 
	1. Moral nihilism (we will not cover this)
	2. Ethical Relativism 

2.Two kinds of Ethical Relativism
-----------------------------------
- individual 
- cultural 

Indicidual Relativsm - aka Ethical Subjectivism or simply subjectivism
- denies the existence of an objective set of ethical standards beyond our own individual view 
- to say that something is ethically wrong (eg. torture, abortion, etc.) is simply to say "i disapprove". 

Cultural Relativism 
- ethic and values come from and depends on ones culture
- denies existence of an objective, culture independent set of ethical standards
- to say something is morally wrong is simply to say "we disapprove around here"

3.Some Implications of ES and CR
--------------------------
a.Moral Infallibility 
- with form of relativism, anything you or your culture decide is right must be morally correct 
- for cultural relativism, Culture is right even if individual views differently. Problematic since cultures have been mistaken in the past  (eg. Slavery). How to account for the iconclasts who go against the culture and by doing so, change or improve it? 

b. Moral Equivalence 
- ones views or those of ones culture are just as right as the views of others. 

Argument in favor of relativism. 
- Both forms can facilitate tolerance and open mindedness.
- sometimes its very hard to find an objective standard to setlle differences. (eg. Topic of abortion in US). Relatvisim doesnt require a standard

Arguments against Relativism
- The most we can say about Horrendous or evil acts is that " I dont approve" (Subjectivism) or "Thats not how we do things around here" (Cultural Relativism) Such a weak stance does not sit well with most people.
