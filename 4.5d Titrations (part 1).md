**Titration** - an application of solution stoichiometry in which a solution of known concentration is used to analyse a solution of unknown concentration. Two most common types are acid-base titrations and oxidation-reduction titration. 

### Acid-base titrations 

used in analytical laboratories to accurately determine the concentration of an acid or base solution or the molar mass of an unknown acid or base. 

An acid-base **indicator**,a dye that shows by a change in color when the acid-base reaction is complete, is added to the solution in the flask before the titration is begun. Most common acid-base indicator used is phenolphtaleinm, an organic dye that is colorless in acidic solutions and pink in basic solutions. 

**Equivalence point**  in a acid-base titration is the point at which the acid (or base) in the flask has been consumed completely by the base (or acid) that has been added from the buret. That is [H+] = [OH-] at the equivalence point in an acid base titration.

Standardization another type of acid-base titration, the concentration of an acid or a base is determined accurately by the use of either a primary standard or another solution whose concentration has already been determined. 

primary standard - an acid or base that can be obtained in a very pure form such as potassium hydrogen phtalate(KHC8H4O4) or sodium carbonate (Na2CO3)
