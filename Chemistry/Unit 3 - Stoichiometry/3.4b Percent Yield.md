Labortory resuts rarely match calculations exactly, we make a distinction between the predicted amount of product, called the **Theoretical yield** and the actual amount of product produced in the experiment, called the **Experimental yeld**(or actual yield). 

$$Percent\;yield=\frac{experimental\;yield}{theoretical\;yield}*100\%$$
### Example of Calculating percent yield 
Consider the resction of $Pb(NO_3)_2$ with $NaCl$ to form $PbCl_2$ and $NaNO_3$.
if 24.2g $Pb(NO_3)_2$ is reacted with excess $NaCl$ and 17.3g of $PbCl_2$ is ultimatel isolated, what is the percient yield for the reaction. 

1. Write a balanced chemical equation for the rection 
$$Pb(NO_3)_2+2NaCl\;\rightarrow\;PbCl_2+2NaNO_3$$

2. Use the amount of limiting reactant to calculate the theoretical yield of $PbCl_2$
$$24.2gPb(NO_3)_2*\frac{1molPb(NO_3)_2}{331.2g}*\frac{1molPbCl_2}{1molPb(NO_3)_2}*\frac{278.1g}{1molPbCl_2}=20.3PbCl_2$$
3. use the experimental yield (17.3g PbCl_2) and the theoretical yield (20.3gPbCl_2) to calculate the percent yield for the reaction. 
$$\frac{17.3gPbCl_2}{20.3gPbCl_2}*100\%=85.2\%$$
Is answer reasonable? The theoretical yield is greater than the experimental yield, so the percent yield is less than 100%
