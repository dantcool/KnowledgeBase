A balanced chemical reaction shows the relative amounts of reactants and products involved a chemical reaction on both the molecular and macroscopic scale. Heres an example of the balanced equation for the reaction of metan with oxygen to form carbon dioxide and water describes the reaction at the atomic level and at the macroscopic level.

On the molecular level, 1 molecule of CH4 reacts with 2 molecules of O2 to produce one molecule of CO2 and 2 molecules of H2O. Scaling up the macroscopic amounts, the equation also represents the reaction of 1 mole of CH4(16.04g) with 2 moles of O2(64.00g) to form 1 mole of CO2(44.01g) and 2 moles of H20(36.03g).

Reaction stoichiometry is the study of the relationships between the amount of reactants and products on the meacroscopic scaled and balanced chemical equations are the key to undestanding these relationships. 

Consider the reaction between butane and oxygen to from carbon dioxide and water the balanced equation is. 
$$2C_4H_{10}+13O_2\rightarrow8CO_2+10H_2O$$
According to the balanced equation 8 mol of CO2 are produced for every 2 mole of C4H10 that react with oxygen. A typical reaction however might involve more or fewer than 2 mol of butane. The balanced equation gives us the information needed to determine the amount of CO2 produced from any amount of C4H10. 

The mole to mole relationships in a balanced chemical equation are used in the form of conversion factors to relate amounts of material reacting or forming during a chemical reaction. 

Example consider a butane lighter that contains 0.24 mol of C4H10. We can use the coefficients in the balanced equation to determine the amount of carbon dioxide produced in the reaction of this amount of butane with oxygen. 

$$2C_4H_{10}+13O_2\rightarrow8CO_2+10H_2O$$
$$0.24mol\;C_4H_{10}*\frac{8mol\;CO_2}{2mol\;C_4H_{10}} = 0.96mol\;CO_2$$


the ratio of coefficients from the balanced equation, called the **Stoichiometric Factor** (or stoichiometric ratio) relates the amount of one specis to another. In this case the stoichiometric factor relates the amount of carbon dioxide (CO2) formed from the reaction of butane with oxygen. Its important to note the stoichiometric factors are always created from coefficients in a balanced chemical equation. 

The amount of water produced in the reaction can also be determined. Again use the stoichiometric factor by using the coefficients in the balanced euation that relate moles of H2O and C4H10. 

$$2C_4H_{10}+13O_2\rightarrow8CO_2+10H_2O$$
$$0.24mol\;C_4H_{10}*\frac{10mol\;H_2O}{2mol\;C_4H_{10}} = 1.20mol\;H_2O$$

Stoichiometric relationships exist between all species in a balnced equation. For example if the reaction of a sample of butane with oxygen produces 2.8 mol of CO2 its possible to determine the amount of H2O produced using a stoichiometric factor 

$$2.8mol\;CO_2*\frac{10mol\;H_2O}{8mol\;CO_2} = 3.5mol\;H_2O$$

Molar mass is combined with stoichiometric factors in order to detemine the mass of each species consumed or produced in the reaction In these types of calculations its important to keep track of the units for each value calculate. 

Example- the mass of oxygen consumed by 7.8g of C4H10 is calulated below

First the mass of butane is converted into units of mole
$$7.8gC_4H_{10}*\frac{1mol\;C_4H_{10}}{58.1g}=0.13mol\;C_4H{10}$$
Next use the stoichiometric factor from the balanced equation to convert moles of C4H10 to moles of O2.

$$0.13mol\;C_4H_{10}*\frac{13mol\;O_2}{2mol\;C_4H_{10}}=0.87mol\;O_2$$

Finally use the molar mass of oxygen to calculate the mass of O2 consumed by 7.8g of C4H10

$$0.87mol\;O_2*\frac{32.0g}{1mol\;O_2} = 28gO_2$$
