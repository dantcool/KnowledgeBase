The emprical formula of a compound may not be representative of the molecular formula of a compound. For example the hydrocarbons cyclopropane and cyclobutane has the same Emprical formula (CH2) but different molecular formulas (C3H6 and C4H8)

The **Molecular Formula** of a compound is a whole-number multiple of its empirical formula. For cyclobutane, the empricial formula(CH2) has a molar mass of 14.04g/mol and the molecular formal has a molar mass of 56.11g/mol 

$$\frac{Molar\;mass\;of\;compound}{molar\;mass\;of\;empirical\;formula}=Whole\;Number$$
$$\frac{56.11g/mol}{14.03g/mol}=3.99\approx4$$
The whole number multiple is equal to the number of the empirical formula units in one molecule of the compound. For cyclobutane, there are four empirical formulas units per molecule, and the molecular formulas is (CH2)4 or C4H8. the molar mass of a compound must be dtermined, usually through additional expirements, in order to determine the molecular formula of a compound.
