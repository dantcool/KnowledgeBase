A balanced equation is the situation in which a reaction and all of the reactants are converted into products. Under Nonstoichiometric conditions the reaction cotinues only until one of the reactants is entirely consumed and at thi point the reaction stops. 

### Example
_____________________________________
When a butane lighter is lit only a small amount of butane is released from the lighter and is combined with the much larger amount of oxygen in the air.  
$$2C_4H_10+13O_2 \rightarrow 8CO_2+10H_2O$$
The oxygen is considered an **Excess Reactant** because of how much is present for the butane to react with. The butane is the **Limiting Reactant** because it controls the amount of products produced in the reaction. 

### Example of identifying limiting reactants(Mole ratio method)
_______________________________
Identify the limiting reactant in the reaction of hydrogen and oxygen to form water if 61.0g of $O_2$  and 8.40g of $H_2$ are combined. Determine the amount (in grams) of excess reactant that remains after the reaction is complete

1. write the balanced quemical equation 
	$$2H_2+O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$$
2. Deterine the limiting reactant by comparing the relative amounts of reactants available. Calc the amount (in mol) of one of the reactants needed and compare that value to the amount available 
$$amount\;of\;0_2\;needed:\; 8.40gH_2\frac{1molH_2}{2.016g}\frac{1molO_2}{2molH_2}=2.08molO_2$$
$$amount\;of\;O_2\;available:\;61.0gO_2*\frac{1molO_2}{32.0g}=1.91molO_2$$
more $O_2$ is needed (2.08mol) than is available (1.91mol) so $O_2$ is the limiting reactant

3. Use the amount of the limiting reactant $O_2$ available to calculate the amount of excess $H_2$ needed for complete reaction 
$$1.91molO_2\frac{2molH_2}{1molO_2}\frac{2.016g}{1molH_2}=7.70gH_2$$
4. calculate the mass of excess reactants that remains when all the limiting reactat is consumed 
$$8.40gH_2\;Available - 7.70g\;consumed=0.70gH_2\;remains$$
Is answer reasonable: More $O_2$ is needed than is available, so it is the limiting reactant. There is $H_2$ remaining afer the reaction is complete because it is the exess reactant in the reaction. 

### Example of identifying limiting reactants (Maximum product Method)
_________________________________
Consider the reaction of gold with nitric acid and hydrochloric acid 
$$Au+3HNO_3+4HCL \rightarrow HAuCl_4+3NO_2+3H_2O$$
Determine the limiting reactant in a mixture containing 125g of ach reactant and calculate the maximum mass )in grams) of $HAuCl_4$ that can be produced in the reaction. 

In this case there are 3 reactants. So it is most efficient to calculate the maximum amount of product ($HAuCl_4$ ) that can be produced by each one. The reactant that produces the least amount of $HAuCl_4$ is the limiting reactant. 

1. Write the balanced equation 
$$Au+3HNO_3+4HCL\;\rightarrow\;HAuCL_4+3NO_2+3H_2O$$
2. Use the molar mass of each reactant and the stoichiometric factors derived from the balanced equation to calculate the amount of $HAuCl_4$ that can be produced by each rectant.
$$125\text{ g Au} \left(\frac{1\text{ mol Au}}{197.0\text{ g}}\right) \left(\frac{1\text{ mol HAuCl}_4}{1\text{ mol Au}}\right) \left(\frac{339.8\text{ g}}{1\text{ mol HAuCl}_4}\right) = 216\text{ g HAuCl}_4$$
