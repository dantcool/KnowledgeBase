The analysis of a mixture is a common process performed in the chemical laboratory. For exmaple and environmental sample containing a mixture of compounds can be analyzed for a single compound by first chemically isolating the compound of interest, followed by chemical analysis to determine the amount of compound in the sample. This type of analysis uses the stoichiometric methods developed to determine the purity of a chemical sample or the chemical makeup of the sample. Example below 

### Example of using stoichiometric methods to analyze a mixture

A soil sample is analyzed to determine the iron content. First the iron is isolated as Fe(NO3)2. This compound is reacted with KMnO4

$$5Fe(NO_3)_2\;\;+KMnO_4\;+\;8HNO_3\;\rightarrow\;5Fe(NO_3)_3+Mn(NO_3)_2+4H_2O+KNO_3$$
in one experiment a 12.2 g sample of soil required 1.73g of KMnO4 to react completely with the Fe(NO3)2. Determine the percent (by mass) of iron present in the soil sample. 

- Asked to calculate the mass percent of iron in a soil smaple 
- Given a balanced equation, The mass of solid analyzed, the mass of one of the reactant used to analyze the sample. 

**Step 1** Use the mass of KMnO4 to determine the amount of Fe(NO3)2 present in the soil sample

$$1.73 gKMnO_4(\frac{1molKMnO_4}{158.0g})(\frac{5molFe(NO_3)_2}{1molKMnO_4})=0.0547molFe(NO_3)_2$$
**Step 2** use the stoichiometry of the chemical formula of Fe(NO_3)_2 and the molar mass of Fe to calculate the mas of Fe present in the soil sample. 

$$0.0547 Fe(NO_3)_2(\frac{1molFe}{1molFe(NO_3)_2})(\frac{55.85g}{1molFe})=3.06gFe$$
**Step 3** use the mass of Fe in the soil sample and the mass of the soil sample to calculate the percent by mass of Fe in the soil. 

$$\frac{3.06gFe\;in\;soil\;sample}{12.2\;soil\;sample}*100\%=25.1\%Fe$$
Is answer reasonable? Because the soil sample is not pure, the mass of iron in the soil is less than the mass of the soil sample and the weight percent of iron in the sample is less than 100%
