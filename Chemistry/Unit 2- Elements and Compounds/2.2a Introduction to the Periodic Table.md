The periodic table contains information of every element and organizes the elements according to their physical and chemical properties. 

Each entry represents a single element and contains
- elements chemical symbo
- atomic number
- averagne atomic weight 

The elements are arranged in vertical columns called groups and horizontal rows called periods.

Group 1A - alkali metals - All shiny slids that react vigorously with air, water, and halogens
Group 2A - alkaline earth metals - metals that react with water to form alkaline solutions
Group 6A - chalcogens
Group 7A - Halogens
Group 8A - Noble gases - Least reactive eements in the periodic table

There are 7 horizontal periods in the periodic table. Portions of period 6 and 7 are below the main body of the periodic table with the special names **Lanthdanides** and **Actinides**

Elements on the left side of the periodic table are **Metals** Elements on the right side are **NonMetals** and the elements at the interface of the two regions are **Metalloids** and **Semimetals**. 
- With the exception of mercure being a liquid metal at room temperature metals are generally shiny solids that are ductiole and good conductors of electricity. 
- Nonmetals are generally dull brittle solids or gases that do not conduct electricity; bromine is the only liquid nonmetal at room temperature. 
- Metalloids have both propertis of Metals and Nonmetals

Most of the elements in the periodic table are solids. 
- only 2 exists as liquid at room temperature(mercury and Bromine)
- 11 elements are gases at room temperature(hydrogen, nitrogen, ocygen, fluorine, chlorine, helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, and radon)
- Elements found as individual atoms are Helium, Sodium, and Mercury
- Element exisitng as molecules consisting of two or more atoms are Oxygen O2, Sulfur S8, and White Phosphorus P4
- Elements with an extended three-dimensional network of connected atoms (Silicon, carbon, red phosphorus)
- 7 elements exist as diatomic molecules in their most stable form (H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2, and I2)

Red Phosuphorus (P) and White  Phosphorus (P4) are examples of **allotropes**, Forms of the same element that differ in their physical and chemical properties. More examples include oxygen(O2), Triatomic ozone (O3) and carbon.
