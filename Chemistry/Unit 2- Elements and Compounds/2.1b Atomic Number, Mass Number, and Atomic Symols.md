
Atoms of each elements can be distingushed by the number of protons in the nucleus. 
The **ATOMIC NUMBER** of an elements is equal to the number of protons in the nucleus. 

Example - a carbon atom has six protons in its nucleus, and therefore its atomic number is 6 (Z=6).

Each element has a unique atomic number and all atoms of that element have the same number of protons in the nucleus. Because protons carry a positive charge of (+1), in a neutral atom the atomic number also equals the number of electons (-1 charge) in that atom. 

An atom can also be charactarized by its mass. The mass of an atom measured in **atomic mass units (u)** is essentialy equal to the number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom, called the **mass number (A)** which the sum of the number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom of an element.  

Example - a carbon atom with 6 protons and 6 neutrons in its nucleas has a mass number of 12 (A=12). A gold atom with 79 protons and 119 neutrons in its nucleus has a mass numbe of 198(A=198). 

**Atomic Symbol** - a one or two letter symbol that represents the element along with its atomic number written as a subscript number, the mass number written as the superscript number.
