### Recognizing Oxidation-Reduction reactions 
We can generally predict the products of acid-base and precipitation reactions. This is not the case for oxidation-reduction reaction. In these reaction, predicting the products is beyond the scope of the intro to organic chemistry. That being said you should be able to recognize an oxidation-reduction reaction. 

Oxidation-reduction reactions always involve a change in the oxidation number of one or more atoms. Therefore the most certain means of determining whether a reaction is an oxidation-reduction reaction is to determine the oxidation numbers of all elements in the reaction. If any of the oxidation numbers change when proceeding from reactants to product the reaction is an oxidation-reduction -reaction.  

Consider the following reaction 
$$Cl_2(g)+H_3AsO_4(aq)\;\rightarrow\;HAsO_2(aq)+2HClO(aq)$$

Any element with a change in oxidation number when proceeding from reactant to products has been oxidized or reduced. In the preceding reaction, the oxidation number of chlorine increases from 0 (in Cl2) to +1 (inHClO). The oxidation number has increased because each chlorin atom has lost an electron. Chlorine Cl_2 has been oxidized to HClO and is the reducing agent. The oxidation number of arsenic decreasses from +5 (in H3AsO4) to +3 (in HAsO2) The oxidation number has decreased because each arsen atom has gained two electrons. H3AsO4 has been reduced to HAsO2 and is the oxdizing agent.
