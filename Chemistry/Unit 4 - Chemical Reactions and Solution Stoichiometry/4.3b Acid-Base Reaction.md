Acid-base reactions are a second example of double displacement (exchange) reactions that take place in aqueous solution. Acids and bases are common materials, found in foods(citric acid gives lemons their  sour taste, acetic acid is one component of vinegar) and household materials (sodium hydroxide and ammonia are bases found in many cleaning products) There are many ways to define acids and bases. In one of the simplest definitins, an **Acid** is a species that produces H+ ions when dissolved in water and a **Base** is a species that increases the amount of OH- ions in a solution. Bases also react with H+ ions and can therefore also be described as proton(H+) acceptors. Acids and bases are electrolytes that produce hydrated ions in aqueous solution. 

An acid-base reaction is also known as neutralization reaction because the reaction can result in a solution that is neutral. That is no longer has acidic or basic properties. For example consider the reaction of nitric acid (HNO_3) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH)

$$HNO_3(aq)/acid\;\;+NaOH(aq)/base\;\rightarrow\;H_2O(l)/water+NaNO_3(aq)/salt$$
the reaction between an acid and a base produces water and a **Salt** an ionic compound formed as the result of an acid-base reaction that consist of a cation donated from a base and an anion donated from an acid. Notice that this is also a double displacement reaction. The base in this reaction $NaOH$ acts as a proton acceptor, combining with H+ to form water.

###  Bases
_____________________
Bases are also characterized as being either strong or weak (Table 4.3.3) All comon strong bases are hydroxid salts such as NaOH and KOH. Weak bases are most often compounds containing a nitrogen atom such as ammonia NH_3 and trimethylamine (CH_3)_3N

![[Pasted image 20261013173619.png|392]]

When added to water **Strong Bases** act as strong electrolytes and are ionized completely. For example when NaOH is added to water, it forms hydrated Na+ and OH- ions

$$NaOH(s)\frac{100\%}{ionized}>Na^+(aq)+OH^-(aq)$$
When added to water, weak bases also increase the concentration of hydroxide ion (OH-)but do so by reacting with water. For example NH_3 is a weak base and reacts with water to form NH_4+ and OH- ions

$$NH_3(g)+H_2O(l)\frac{less\;than\;100\%}{ionized}>NH_4^+(aq)+OH^-(aq)$$
like a weak acid, a **weak base** is a weak electrolyte that does not completely ionize.In a solution of NH_3, for example, only about 1% of the NH_3 molecules react with water. 

### Net ionic Equation for Acid-base Reactions 
____________________
The net ionic equation for strong-acid base reaction is written by first writing a completely ionized equation. Consider the reaction between nitric acid and sodium hydroxide. 

$$H^+(aq)+NO_3^-(aq)+Na^+(aq)+OH^-(aq)\;\rightarrow\;H_2O(l)+Na^+(aq)+NO_3^-(aq)$$
Identifying the spectator ions that remain unchaged during the reaction ($Na^+ and NO_3^-$) and removing them results in the net ionic equation for most strong acid-strong base reactions. 

$$H^+(aq)+OH^-(aq)\;\rightarrow\;H_2O(l)$$
_____________________________________
The net ionic equation for the reaction of strong acids or bases with weak acids or bases also involves writing a completely ionized equation and identifying any spectator ions. For example consider the reaction of hydrochloride acid with weak base ammonia. 

$$HCl(aq)+NH_3(aq)\;\rightarrow\;NH_4Cl(aq)$$
In this reaction, the base acts as a proton acceptor, accepting H+ from the acid to form the ammonium ion, NH_4+. Writing the equation in its completely ionized form.
$$H^+(aq)+Cl^-(aq)+NH_3(aq)\;\rightarrow\;NH_4^+(aq)+Cl^-(aq)$$
Ammonium chloride,NH_4Cl, is a soluble ionic compund that dissociates completely in aqueous solution. Removing the spectator ion (Cl-) results in the following net ionic equation.
$$H^+(aq)+NH_3(aq)\;\rightarrow\;NH_4^+(aq)$$
