A third double displacement reaction commonly encountered in the laboratory is the reaction of an ionic compound with an acid to form a gas. These gas-forming reactions introduced previously in this unit are a special type of acid-base reaction involving less obvious bases such as $CuCo_3$ and $Na_2SO_3$ (figure 4.3.3)
![[Pasted image 20261013190713.png|353]]
When the gas forming reaction involves a metal carbonate, one product of the reaction is carbon dioxide gas. Consider the reaction between copper(II) carbonate and hydrochloric acid. 

$$CuCo_3(s)+2HCl(aq)\;\rightarrow\;CuCl_2(aq)+H_2CO_3(aq)$$
As shown previously on product of the double displaceent reaction $H_2CO_3$ undergoes a decomposition reaction to produce gaseous carbon dioxide and water 

$$H_2CO_3(aq)\;\rightarrow\;H_2O(l)+CO_2(g)$$
The net reaction therefore is 

$$CuCO_3(a)+2HCl(aq)\;\rightarrow\;CuCl_2(aq)+CO_2(g)+H_2O(l)$$
The procedure of writing net ionic equations for gas-forming rections is similar to that used for precipitation and acid-base net ionic equations as shown in table 4.3.5

When a gas-forming reaction involves an ionic compound that is not a metal carbonate, it is not always easy to predict the products of the reaction. For examle, sodium sulfite, $NA_2SO_3$ reacts with hydrochloric acid to form gaseous sulfur dioxide.

$$Na_2SO_3(aq)+2HCl(aq)\;\rightarrow\;2NaCl(aq)+SO_2(g)+H_2O(l)$$
The net ionic equation for this reaction is 
$$SO_3^{2-}(aq)+2H^+(aq)\;\rightarrow\;SO_2(g)+H_2O(l)$$
