**nodes** - regions of space where there is zero probability of finding an electron (2types)
1. Planar nodes(AKA angular nodes and sometimes take the shape of a cone)
2. Radial nodes(AKA spherical nodes)

- planar nodes are found in all orbitals other than the s orbitals 
- each p orbital has a planar node that is perpendicular to the axis where there are regions of high density 
- d orbitals generally have two planar nodes that are perpendicular to each other 
Example 
$3d_{xy}$ orbital has two planar nodes, one in the xz plane and one in the yz plane 
number of planar nodes in a subshell is given by the value of the angular momentum quantum number, l


| l   | subshell | # of planar nodes |
| --- | -------- | ----------------- |
| 0   | s        | 0                 |
| 1   | p        | 1                 |
| 2   | d        | 2                 |
| 3   | f        | 3                 |
