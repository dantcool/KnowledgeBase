Standard heat of formation values, when combined with Hess's law are used to calculate a **standard enthalpy of Reaction**(delta h rxn) 

EXAMPLE 
consider the following reaction at 25C 
$$2NaHCO_3\rightarrow Na_2CO_3+CO_2+H_2O$$
2 steps 
First reactants are broken down into constituent elements in thir standard states 
Second the elements are recombined to form products 

![[Pasted image 20261106022204.png]]
Both steps invovle deltah_f values. In  step 1 the sign of delta h_f[NAhCO_3(s)] is reversed because the standard heat of formation reaction is reverse. The values is also multiplied by 2 because 2 mol NaHCO_3 is broken down int constituent elements. Step 2 reactions are standard heat of formation reaction so enthalpy change for each is equl to delta h_f

summing the 4 reactions in step 1 and 2 results in the net reaction. Using hess's law the enthalp change for the net reactions is equal to the sum of the enthalpy changes for the reaction in steps 1 and 2
