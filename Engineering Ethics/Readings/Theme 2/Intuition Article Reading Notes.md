Section 3 (Defending the reliability of Intuitons)- David sosas defense of the reliabilty of intuitions 
----------------
- David sosa recognises the attacks of psychology studies on why intuition is not as reliable as one thinks but rejects the notion that all inutitive judgements are unrealiable. Sosa argues that inutive judgement is comparable to introspective and perceptive judgements. Most people are willing to treeat both introspection and perception as generally reliable sources of informtion of justified knowlede but under certain conditoons both are predictably misleading. Examle when one is driving down a road in the summer one will sometimes pervcieve that there is water on the road at a distance even thugh ecperience dictates otherwise the misleading perception remains. 
- within a certain rang both faculties accurately track the world be it ones internal state or ones external surroundings. Perception and introsepction can be treated as reliavvle sources of knowlede even in well established limitations why cant we think the same of intutions. 
- Sosa - "perception is systematically misleadin in well known circumstances yet we do not dismiss perception because of that. We do not conclude that one is blind just because one is color blind. We do not dismiss that one is blind altogether simply because it is subject to systematic illusions"
- Sosa mainains that inuitions should be treated as reliable under certain conditions because intuitive judgment are not any more flawed than introspective or perceptive judgements. But for the conditions to be set one needs to define such conditions under which intuitions are seen as being trustworthy to establish a range of reliability but currently there is no unified theory to explain intuition itself, their source, and range of reliability
