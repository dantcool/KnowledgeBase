Question : why study systems in an ethics course?
Answere: Ethics is complex, involving open ended question and challenges. Ethics is a method for engaging in complexity. Systems theory studies complexity, attempting to understand and manage it. 

Complexity occurs when systems interact in multiple ways, each following its own rules, which results in randomness, non-linearity, hierarchy,collective dynamics, emergence.

Emergence occurs when a larger system has properties that its subsystems and parts do not have on their own. These properties or behaviors emerge only when the parts interact in a wider whole. The whole is greater than the sum of its parts 
EXAMPLES
- ant colonies exhibit complex behavior without central decision making
- flocking birds (murmuration) or schooling fish
- human societies (economic market, traffic flow/ congestion)

we see systems operating in most ethical scenarios

Engineering and computer science activities exist in wider systems, as do the engineers and computer scientists themselves and the ethical situations they face 

An understanding of systems facilitates effective action. (true in both ethical and other situations)

Defining a system 
--------------------------
3 essential ingredients of any system 
1. **Elements**(aka variable,compnents)
	1. Physical 
		- Reservoir (for water)
		- Pipes 
		- Pumps
		- Water 
	2. Non-Physical(Sometimes abstract)
		- Software
		- Information(Data)
		- People(The roles they play,the work they do)
		- Models 
		Genreral Features 
	Specific/discrete elements can be interchanged without destroying or fundamentally changing the system
2. **interconnetions** 
	- Exist between elements
	-  also exists between subsystems
	- involve cause relationships(importnat concept fo the causal loop diagrams,which focus on the interconnections)
	- like elements, they can be either 
		1. Physical:Flow of water between reservoir and treatment system
		2. Non physical : flow of information between water sensor and control system 
	- Unlike elemtns(generally speaking), changes in interconnections can have significant impact on the system(affecting its healthand persistence) Example: insufficient water flow can overheat and destroy pumps 
3. **function/purpose**
	- Distinction between these 2 words
		- natural systems have a function 
		- Artificial Systems(human-crafted) have a purpose implies intentionality
	- What about unintended behaviors in an artificial system?
		- Function or purpose 
	- Function/Purpose is determined by te actual system beheavior not neccesarily what was intended. In other words a systems actual behavior determines its fuction/Purpose. 
		- Systems have a tendency to self-perpetuate(negative entropy)
		- "System behaviors need not be human purposes and are not necessarily those intended by any single actor withing the system. The purpose of subunits may add up to an overall behavior that no one wants"(Meadows,2008,p.15)
