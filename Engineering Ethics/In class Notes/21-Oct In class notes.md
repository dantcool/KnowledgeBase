Objections and Responses to Aristotelian Virtue ethics 
-----------------
Objection 1 : this is just simple egoism 
A virtuous person is motivated to act virtuously for their own happiness
	
Response 1a: Its true that sometimes at a general level virtues benefit their possessors on particular occasions. However the motive is usually focused externally (on the subject of the action)

Example: Kindness - Are you only kind to others because you think it will help you?

Response 1b: There are times when a virtuous person concern for others will lead to the sacrifice of  their own interest.
_______________________________________________

Objection 2: 	Virtue Ethics is a sophistical form of egoism 
The virtuous persons motive are still their own Happiness/Eudaimonia.(maybe trading shorter-term or physical forms for longer term or non-physical forms)

Response 2a: Yes, that is possible and maybe that is ok. We can only live our own lives and truly know our own motivations. It could be egoism for some people (maybe the "Best" form of egoism)

Response 2b: same as 1b above (i.e sacrificing even longer-term self-interest ) 
________________________

Objection 3: What about the case of moral saints. AKA the miserable charity worker (MCW)
The miserable charity worker seems virtuous but is not happy. 

Response 3a: maybe the MCW is actually happy 
periods of temporary relative unhappiness but happiness trends upward

Response 3b: Virtues benefit their possessor much or some of the time, but not all the time. Sometimes being virtuous involves sacrifice .

Response 3c: Maybe the MCW is not actually being virtuous (see below)

Motivations and Virtue
-----------------------------------
Virtue requires the right motivation (internalized, correct/appropriate emotions )

We need to want to do the virtuous thing for the act to be considered virtuous. 

Forcing ourselves to do something only for external reasons might demonstrate one virtue,self control, or maybe fanaticism in the extreme. but not virtue in the broad sense of virtue ethics. 

Motives- why they matter
----------------------------
- creating harmony between our motives and our actions this increases satisfaction 
- internalization increases reliability 
- focus is on character over mere rule following
- Consequences don't paint the whole moral picture : chance or luck may also play a role and sometimes things are outside of our control. Surely motivations and intentions count for something in such cases.
