**_Applying Virtue Ethics_  
**There are multiple ways to frame the positive and negative implications of decisions made in a case. In this assignment, you will use virtue ethics to do so. A useful and important place to begin is by examining how the eudaimonia of the stakeholders involved is affected. Some might be negatively affected whereas others might experience positive outcomes. Often such impacts can enhance or interfere with their well-being / flourishing / Happiness (eudaimonia). 

**_Workflow for Creating a CLD (recommended):_** 

1. Identify the most significant variables in the case. This can include stakeholders, raw material sources, broader social impacts, environmental effects, etc.  A range of 7-15 variables is strongly recommended.   
     
2. Where they exist, identify the causal relationships between variables including the important details of direction of influence and polarity. Is the relationship between variables A and B, for example, unilateral? Bilateral? Does A act proportionally on B (i.e., increasing A causes B to increase, or decreasing A causes B to decrease)?  Or does A act Inversely on B (i.e., increasing A causes B to decrease, or decreasing A causes B to increase)?  Indicate direction of influence using an arrowhead and clarify polarity using the following conventions:   
      a) A proportional causal relationship is represented by a blue* line with a positive sign at the arrowhead.   
      b) An inverse causal relationship is represented by a red* line with a negative sign at the arrowhead.   
    Go through this process of identifying and labeling for each and every instance where there is a causal relationship between variables.  
3. Identify all feedback loops and classify/label these as either reinforcing (R) or balancing (B) as appropriate. Feedback loops should be labeled in the center of the loop where possible and indicate what type of feedback loop it is (R or B).  In cases where there is more than one feedback loop of a given type, differentiate these using numbers (e.g., R1, R2, R3, etc.). TIP: Make sure a feedback loop actually exists before labeling it (i.e., check that the arrow heads point a pathway back to the start).  
4. Identify and label the major delays associated with causal relationships using two parallel lines across (i.e., perpendicular to) the relevant arrow. Delays are important to identify when thinking about the dynamics of the system (note: the significance and inclusion of a delay will depend on the temporal scale or "time horizon" of your system model).   
      
5. Review your CLD for any potential system archetypes. Do you see any familiar patterns? Or can you think of possibilities where one could develop based on your research?  If so, identify these and include them in your written analysis. Refer to the required readings from Theme 3, Unit 3 (especially Kim's "System Archetypes at a Glance" and Meadow's Chapter 5) as well as your notes from the relevant lectures.   
     
6. Review your CLD for potential leverage points. Refer to the required reading from Theme 3, Unit 2 by Meadows on Leverage Points (also in Chapter 6 of the optional book by Meadows).   
     
7. Approach these "steps" iteratively/recursively (remember, while this is laid out sequentially, it is non-linear and iterative in practice).
